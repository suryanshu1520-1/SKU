# NATIONALISM

## I: Nationalism in the West

Man's history is being shaped according to the difficulties it encounters, and the race facilities it discovers for solving them. The history of India has been one of social adaptation, rather than political dominance. India's problem has been the race problem, the problem of living together with differences.

In the West, however, the political mind has developed at the cost of the spiritual and moral nature of man. A nation, in the sense of the political and economic union of a people, is that aspect which a whole population assumes when organized for a mechanical purpose. Society as such has no utilitarian purpose; it is an end in itself; it is a spontaneous expression of man as a social being. It is a natural regulation of human relationships, so that men can develop ideals of life in cooperation with one another.

The Nation of the West is an organization of power, and therefore its science and its organizations are magnificent; but its creative human personality is dwarfed. Its machinery is designed to produce results, and it moves forward with a relentless momentum that crushes everything spontaneous and sacred in human life.

When this engine of organization begins to dominate society, men begin to believe that the nation is the highest entity, that everything must be sacrificed to its power and prestige. This is the idolatry of the nation. It turns man into a specialized component of an enormous machine. It fosters greed and aggression under the guise of patriotism.

Nationalism is a cruel epidemic of evil that is sweeping over the human world of the present age, eating into its moral vitality. It turns neighbors into enemies, fills the world with suspicion and hatred, and turns the sacred earth into an armed camp.

---

## II: Nationalism in Japan

Japan has startled the world with the rapid assimilation of Western techniques and science. She has shown that she can build navies, organize armies, and establish modern industries as efficiently as any European power.

Yet, I tell the people of Japan: Do not mistake the imitation of the West for true strength. The true strength of Asia has never been in the machine of the Nation, but in the spiritual harmony of the community. In borrowing the outer armor of the West, be careful that you do not take in the venom of its aggressive nationalism.

A nation that relies solely upon power is like a monster that grows enormous limbs at the expense of its heart and brain. In the end, the weight of its own weapons crushes it to death. Japan must hold fast to her ancient ideals of beauty, simplicity, and moral refinement, and give to the world an example of how modern power can be spiritualized by moral wisdom.

## III: Nationalism in India

Our real problem in India is not political, but social. It is no use crying for political freedom when our society is ridden with the worst forms of social tyranny. We have compartmentalized our people into castes, denying human dignity to millions, and yet we imagine that we can build a free political democracy on such rotten foundations.

Our fight is against the disease of caste and mutual estrangement. We cannot solve this problem by merely imitating the political institutions of the British. The British government in India is an abstract government—it is the Nation as an organization of rule, not a human government. Between the British ruler and the Indian subject there is no human touch; there is only the cold, impersonal operation of law, police, and taxation.

Therefore, our true salvation lies not in setting up an Indian Nation of power on the Western model, but in reclaiming our spiritual heritage of universal fellowship. Patriotism cannot be our final spiritual shelter; my refuge is humanity. I will not buy glass for the price of diamonds, and I will never allow patriotism to triumph over humanity as long as I live.

India has always stood for the unity of man. She has offered hospitality to every race and culture that has crossed her borders. Her mission in history is to teach the world that humanity is one great family, and that the brotherhood of man is greater than the boundaries of any nation.

## IV: The Sunset of the Century

The last sun of the century sets amidst the blood-red clouds of the West and the whirlwind of hatred.
The naked passion of self-love of Nations, in its drunken delirium of greed, is dancing to the clash of steel and the howling verses of vengeance.

The crimson dawn is coming, not from the blood-stained battlefields of power, but from the quiet altars of human love and sacrifice.
Keep watch, India! Let not the temptress of political power lure you away from your true mission.
Bear the cross of humanity with patient courage, and hold high the torch of truth that dispels the darkness of the world.
