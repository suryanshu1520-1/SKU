# KARMA YOGA

## Chapter I: Karma in Its Effect on Character

The word Karma is derived from the Sanskrit Kri, to do; all action is Karma. Technically, this word also means the effects of actions. In connection with metaphysics, it sometimes means the effects of which our past actions were the causes. But in Karma-Yoga we have simply to do with the word Karma as meaning work.

The goal of mankind is knowledge. That is the one ideal placed before us by Eastern philosophy. Pleasure is not the goal of man, but knowledge. Pleasure and happiness come to an end. It is a mistake to suppose that pleasure is the goal. The cause of all the miseries we have in the world is that men foolishly think pleasure to be the ideal to strive for. After a time man finds that it is not happiness, but knowledge, towards which he is going, and that both pleasure and pain are great teachers, and that he learns as much from evil as from good.

As pleasure and pain pass before his soul they leave upon it different pictures, and the result of these combined impressions is what is called man's "character." If you take the character of any man, it really is but the aggregate of tendencies, the sum total of the bent of his mind; you will find that misery and happiness were equal factors in the formation of that character. Good and evil have an equal share in moulding character, and in some instances misery is a greater teacher than happiness.

All knowledge that the world has ever received comes from the mind; the infinite library of the universe is in your own mind. The external world is only the suggestion, the occasion, which sets you to study your own mind. Newton sat under an apple tree, and an apple fell down; this suggested to him to study his own mind, and he found the links of all thoughts which produced the law of gravitation.

Karma-Yoga is the attaining through unselfish work of that freedom which is the goal of all human nature. Every action that is done by us leaves an impression behind it; it produces a tendency. If a man continuously thinks good thoughts and does good actions, his tendencies become good; they force him to do good in spite of himself. When a man has reached this state, his good character is said to be established.

## Chapter II: Each Is Great in His Own Place

According to the Karma-Yogi, the duty of each man is determined by his position in life. No duty is ugly, no duty is impure. He who does even the most ordinary work in the world, unselfishly and with devotion, is as great as the sage who meditates in the forest.

A householder who does his duty honestly, who works for his family without attachment, who feeds the hungry and shelters the stranger, is doing Karma-Yoga just as much as the sannyasin who has renounced all worldly possessions. To the householder, the home is the temple; to the sannyasin, the universe is the home. Neither is higher than the other; each is great in his own place.

The ideal man is he who, in the midst of the greatest silence and solitude, finds the intensest activity, and in the midst of the intensest activity finds the silence and solitude of the desert. He has learnt the secret of restraint, he has controlled himself.

## Chapter III: The Secret of Work

Helping others physically, by removing their physical needs, is indeed great, but the help is greater according as the need is greater and according as the help is far-reaching. If a man's hunger can be removed for an hour, it is helping him; but if you give him knowledge which will enable him to earn his food for life, you help him much more. Spiritual knowledge is the highest of all benefits, because it destroys our miseries forever.

In helping the world, we must always remember that we are helping ourselves. This world was not created for us to come and help it. The world does not need our help. It is an insult to think that we can help the world. It is a dogma of vanity. God is present in every being. What can we do? We can serve Him. The poor man is God Himself coming to our door in that form to give us an opportunity to serve.

Do not figure out whether you will get money or name or praise. Do not figure out whether work will succeed or fail. Work for work's sake. Worship work. Selfishness is the chief cause of all misery. The only true test of good work is that it brings unselfishness. He who can become completely unselfish is the most successful man, though he may live in a cave and not be known to anyone.

## Chapter IV: What Is Duty?

It is necessary to understand what duty is. When we are commanded to do a thing, it is our duty to do it. But that which is duty in one country is not duty in another; that which is duty in one period of life is not duty in another.

Duty is seldom sweet. It is only when love greases its wheels that it runs smoothly; it is a continuous friction otherwise. How else could parents do their duties to their children, husbands to their wives, and vice versa? Duty becomes a joy only when love is at the root.

Do not judge another's duty by your own standard. Every man must take up his own ideal and endeavor to accomplish it; that is a surer way of progress than taking up other men's ideals, which he can never hope to realize. The highest duty is to be unselfish and to hurt no living creature.

## Chapter V: We Help Ourselves, Not the World

Before considering further how to do work, we must recognize that work is an inevitable necessity of life. Constant action is the law of nature. No one can remain without work even for a moment.

We must work continuously, but we must abandon all attachment to work. What does attachment mean? Attachment means looking for results. When you give a coin to a beggar, if you expect him to say "Thank you", you are attached to the work; you are selling your coin for his praise.

The Karma-Yogi asks: "Why should you look for results?" Be like the sun, which gives light and heat to the whole world, without asking anything in return. The sun does not demand our praise. It shines because it is its nature to shine. So work because it is your nature to work, because you are an instrument in the hands of the Divine.

---

## Chapter VI: Non-Attachment Is Complete Self-Abnegation

The basic idea of Karma-Yoga is complete self-abnegation. Just as water on a lotus leaf rolls off without wetting it, so must the Karma-Yogi live in the world without being stained by its actions.

Selfishness is the only sin. That work is good which tends towards unselfishness; that work is bad which tends towards selfishness. He who works without any selfish motive, who works purely out of love for humanity, is the true Yogi.

Give up all fruits of work unto the Lord. Whatever you do, whatever you think, whatever you suffer, offer it up as an oblation. When you work with this attitude, no karma can bind you; work becomes a worship, and action becomes freedom.

---

## Chapter VII: Freedom

All beings, from the lowest insect to the highest angel, are struggling for freedom. This freedom is the goal of all life. Why do we seek knowledge? Because knowledge makes us free. Why do we seek morality? Because virtue frees us from the bondage of our animal appetites.

The whole of nature is a struggle towards freedom. But true freedom cannot be found in the senses, for the senses are limited and bound by time, space, and causation. True freedom is only in the Spirit, which is infinite and immortal.

Karma-Yoga teaches us how to attain this freedom through work. By doing our duty without attachment, by renouncing the fruits of our labor, the mind becomes purified. The ego is dissolved, and the soul realizes its oneness with all existence.

---

## Chapter VIII: The Ideal of Karma-Yoga

The grand ideal of Karma-Yoga is that a man may live in the world and yet not be of the world. He may be the leader of a nation, the general of an army, or the ruler of an empire, and yet remain completely unattached, calm and serene like the depths of the ocean.

He works with all his heart, with all his mind, with all his soul, but he claims nothing for himself. If success comes, he is not elated; if defeat comes, he is not dejected. He knows that both success and defeat are mere waves on the surface of the infinite sea of Being.

This is the secret of work. This is the path of Karma-Yoga. Stand up, be bold, be strong! Take the whole responsibility on your own shoulders, and know that you are the creator of your own destiny. All the power is within you; you can do anything and everything. Believe in that, do not believe that you are weak; do not believe that you are half-crazy lunatics, as most of us do nowadays. Stand up and express the divinity within you!
